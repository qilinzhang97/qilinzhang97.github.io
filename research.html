Job market paper

Gender-biased Dynastic Savings and the Marriage Market: Evidence from China

This paper studies how parents’ investments in their children shape the marriage market in China. Using household survey data, I document that the marriage market is assortative and the parents’ investment mix differs by child gender. In a dynastic model of parental investment and marriage matching, I find that gender-biased investment lowers the marriage rate: removing the gender gap in the cost of education raises it by 1.85 percentage points. As parents grow richer with China’s growth, the model explains 35 percent of the fall in the crude marriage rate between 2010 and 2024 and predicts a further decline. Subsidies to newlyweds raise marriage, whereas equal-sized transfers to parents lower it.
